Learning Match Cut Image Retrieval with a Segmentation Encoder and Synthetic Data

Abstract

A match cut is a video editing technique that creates a seamless transition by joining shots that share visual similarity, presenting content to viewers in a more striking and evocative manner. This requires careful consideration of how to combine individual shots and the effects of their combinations. However, existing image encoders used for match cut retrieval do not explicitly account for spatial relationships among objects, which are critical for identifying visually similar shot pairs. Furthermore, constructing training data for match cut retrieval is difficult in practice, as real-world examples are scarce and building a dataset from real footage raises copyright and data privacy concerns. To address these challenges, we propose a model consist of segmentation model's image encoder, CNN-based Spatial Compression Module(SCM), and a Projection Head(PH), which is desinged to learn suitable representation for match cut image retrieval. We also we constructed a rendering-based synthetic dataset, varying shot size, camera settings, character type and pose, and background to train our model. Quantitative and qualitative evaluations on real images, along with a user study, show that our method achieves high retrieval performance, demonstrating that combining spatial features from a segmentation encoder only with synthetic data is an effective approach to real-world match cut retrieval.
